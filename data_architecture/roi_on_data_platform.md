# Why Measuring ROI on Data Platforms is Hard

Investing in data platforms is no longer optional — modern organizations rely on robust data lakes, pipelines, and analytics platforms to power insights, decisions, and innovation. Yet, despite heavy investments, many leaders struggle to quantify the **return on investment (ROI)** for their data platforms. Why is that?

### When ROI is Easy to Measure

Some technology investments have **direct, visible outcomes**:

* **Applications**: A new ordering portal or mobile app can be measured through user adoption, transaction volume, or revenue generated.
* **Optimization models**: Implementing a supply chain optimization model may reduce wastage by 15%, saving millions directly.

In these cases, it’s straightforward to **tie the investment to a measurable outcome**.

### Data Platform ROI is Diffused

Data platforms, by contrast, **enable multiple downstream use cases** rather than directly producing revenue:

* Datasets may be **queried by analysts**, fed into dashboards, or consumed by data science models.
* Users benefit from the insights, but the **link between platform investment and business impact is indirect**.
* Data platforms also **mitigate risks**:

  * Proper **data privacy and compliance enforcement** can prevent regulatory fines (e.g., GDPR or HIPAA penalties) and reputational damage.
  * **Data quality and governance** reduce errors in analytics, avoiding costly misinformed decisions.

Moreover, once data leaves the platform — exported to external systems, consumed in ML models, or transformed in dashboards — **visibility into its usage and value is lost**.

### Why This Matters

This “diffused value” creates two challenges:

1. **Difficulty in quantifying ROI**: Unlike apps or optimization projects, the impact of a data platform is distributed across teams, departments, and decisions, making it hard to capture in a single metric.
2. **Risk of undervaluation**: Because the returns are indirect, long-term, and often include **risk mitigation**, organizations may perceive their data investments as underperforming — even if they are critical enablers for analytics, ML, compliance, and decision-making.

### A Pragmatic Approach

While we cannot always measure exact ROI, we **can track proxies**:

* Number of datasets consumed
* Users or teams actively querying or reporting on the platform
* Dashboards or ML models relying on platform data
* Evidence of compliance enforcement or reduction in data-related incidents

Even these metrics are **partial**, but they provide visibility into usage patterns and adoption, helping justify continued investment.
