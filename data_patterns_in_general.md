
## Types of Patterns in General Data Analysis

While patterns in **time series data** are typically time-dependent (like trends, seasonality, cycles), patterns also exist in **non-time series data**. In general data analysis, patterns refer to recurring relationships, structures, or behaviors in the data — but not necessarily related to time.

Here are some common types of patterns you’ll encounter:

### 1. Correlations

A **correlation** indicates how strongly two variables are related and whether the relationship is positive or negative.

* **Positive correlation**: Both variables increase together.
* **Negative correlation**: One variable increases while the other decreases.

**Example**:
In sales data, higher **advertising spend** often correlates with increased **sales revenue**.



### 2. Clusters

**Clustering** groups data points into clusters based on similarities in their features.

* It helps in identifying **natural groupings** or **segments** in data.
* Common in customer segmentation, image analysis, anomaly detection, etc.

**Example**:
E-commerce users might be clustered into:

* High-frequency shoppers
* Occasional bargain hunters
* First-time visitors
  This helps in targeted marketing strategies.


### 3. Associations

**Association rules** uncover relationships where the presence of one item implies the presence of another.

* Typically used in **market basket analysis**.
* Helps identify products that are frequently bought together.

**Example**:
“If a customer buys **bread**, they’re likely to buy **butter** with 80% confidence.”
This is a classic association used in retail product placement.




## Summary

Patterns exist everywhere in data — whether or not time is involved.

* **Correlations** show relationships.
* **Clusters** group similar things.
* **Associations** reveal co-occurrences.
